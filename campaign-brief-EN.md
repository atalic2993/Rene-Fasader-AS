### The guarantee

Note to you, not text that goes out: Oliver has not given any guarantee. The right of complaint under the Norwegian Craftsman Services Act applies regardless. The risk reversal in this campaign is therefore the fixed, written price, and it carries the whole job alone.

Here is how you handle this, concretely. Oliver has not given a guarantee, so the campaign promises no guarantee. That is not a hole in the offer, it is a message about where the risk should be taken down instead: the fixed, written price is the risk reversal in this campaign. The homeowner's fear is not that the wall turns out ugly, it is that the bill turns out bigger than agreed. A fixed price in writing before the start answers exactly that fear, and we can say it with full confidence because the company sees the house before it prices it.

So never write the word guarantee or guaranteed in ads or funnel. Use the price certainty, the Arbeidstilsynet approval and the championship title instead. Those three carry the trust alone.

## §3. Angle and message [LOCKED, angle choice]

**Primary angle: "The photographer is coming".**

The homeowner is going to sell the property. The estate agent has set a date for the photo shoot. After that date the house is locked to that image for the whole sales period, no matter what he does to the wall later. It is the hardest deadline that exists in this niche, and it is the homeowner's own.

The critical frame, as a direct message to you: the angle is about a point in time, not about a problem. The homeowner already knows the wall is dirty, he sees it every day. So the copy should give him a date and someone who does the job, not an explanation of what algae is. Mention the problem in one short breath, and use the rest of the space on how the house looks on photo day.

### Message DNA

> **LOCKED, copy verbatim (Norwegian, ships as is):**

>
> *English translation, for understanding only: "The facade wash is finished before the photographer arrives. The whole wall clean, all the way up, in one day, with a fixed price in writing before we start."*

The four things every single piece of copy must include:

1. The word «fasadevask» and the municipality name, in the first visible line, in the largest type.
2. The outcome on photo day: the house looks freshly washed when the pictures are taken.
3. The mechanism in the subtext: own lift, the entire facade clean all the way up, no scaffolding in the garden.
4. Fixed price in writing at the site visit, and the CTA «Fyll ut skjemaet under 👇».

The funnel says exactly the same as the ad, in its own words.

### Open in the market, among local competitors in these five municipalities

- **Property sale as an angle is completely open.** A search for facade washing tied to property sales returned zero active Norwegian advertisers, while four control searches in the same sweep returned between 1 and 180 hits. Nobody is saying this out loud today.
- **The geography in plain words.** Sandvika has zero advertisers on facade washing, Lillestrøm has one, and it sells roofs. Two of five municipalities are practically untouched, and it takes nothing more than the place name standing in the first line.
- **Own lift as an advantage.** Three local advertisers spend money every day telling the same market that a lift means rigging, scaffolding and risk. Nobody owns the other side: the lift reaches every square metre, including where a drone neither reaches nor can scrub.
- **The championship title.** No local competitor has anything close to a national championship in the trade.
- **Slot count scarcity.** Two locals have vague hints of available capacity, both without a number and without a deadline. Nobody uses a concrete slot count.

Five positions are used up here, and they stop the ad on the spot: softwash and «skånsom» ("gentle", thirteen advertisers say it), "free site visit" as a headline (twelve advertisers), housing cooperatives and annual general meetings (the most crowded field in the whole area), a discount percentage in the ad, and "preserve the value of your home".

## §5. Creative direction for the ads [DIRECTION, your craft]

You make the ads. Below is what they should show and what converts in this niche, not a recipe.

What the ads should show, in priority order:

1. **The continuous washing sequence on the gable wall.** We have raw footage where one and the same light gable with horizontal cladding goes from heavy brown and black streaks to completely clean, with the same camera angle, the same shadow and the water jet visible along the way. This is not a before and after pair assembled in editing, it is one recorded sequence where the dirt disappears while the camera stands still. Cut it raw from the original file and let it run without text for the first few seconds.
2. **The half washed rendered wall with the washing head in the shot.** A sharp diagonal separates the dirty grey green from the clean white, and a worker with red gloves holds the washing head in the middle of the action. A half washed surface is the strongest format in this niche, and none of the mapped competitors here use it.
3. **The lift at work against a facade.** This is the only image that makes the mechanism visible instead of claimed.
4. **The people.** The portraits of Oliver, Stian and Astrid are ready. A face beats a symbol when the recipient is 45 to 65.
5. **The close ups of growth in brick joints and on a heavily overgrown wall,** as a short problem opening before the promise arrives.

All the clips and images above have already been pulled and are ready alongside this brief, in the folder `research/materiell`. Keep producing on what we have, and keep the wheels turning without waiting for new material.

Angle types that convert in this niche:

- **Show, do not claim.** People believe what they see. A half washed wall proves without promising anything.
- **One desire per ad.** Never mix property sale, the neighbour and fear of damage in the same creative, pick one and run it clean.
- **Bright side.** Mention the problem in one short breath, and use the rest on how the house looks afterwards.
- **A new image every three seconds** in video, otherwise they scroll past.
- **Video under 20 seconds or over 30,** and stay away from the band in between.

**Design and brand.** The ads should look like Rene Fasader, not like a generic washing ad. The client's own expression, taken straight from their website:

- Primary colour: deep petrol `#0c343d`. This is also the only colour in the logo.
- Accent colour: light sand yellow `#ffe599`.
- Support colours: light grey `#cacaca` on surfaces and edges, dark grey `#757575` on body text.
- Font: GeneralSans in Regular and Semibold.
- Logo: `rene-fasader-logosymbol.svg`, transparent vector file, a tall building made of dashed floor bands. Use it as it is.

There are older ads in navy blue and gold from a previous campaign. Build the new campaign in petrol and sand yellow, which is the client's own palette and the only one that comes from the client themselves. Then the click from ad to landing page feels like the same company.

1. **Hero with proof.** The headline is the same thought as the ad, with the service and the municipality in plain words. ## §7. Target group and area [LOCKED, build exactly]

**Who we are talking to.** A homeowner between 40 and 65 in a small house, meaning a detached house, semi detached house or terraced house, who is about to put the property up for sale and has a photo date from the estate agent in the calendar. He is a done for me buyer who hates friction, does not want to rent equipment or climb himself, and is in the middle of a move where time is the scarcest resource. He is not a bargain hunter, but he is afraid of a bill he was not prepared for. He cares about what the house signals, both to the buyer who is scrolling and to the neighbour who sees it from the road.

The area has 171,331 small houses, and 97,052 of them were built in 1980 or earlier. That means cladding that has stood for at least 45 years and is visibly discoloured after a whole summer. 335,885 people in the area are between 40 and 64.

### Area list

- Oslo
- Lillestrøm
- Asker
- Bærum
- Sandvika

The base is Neuberggata 3B, 0367 Oslo, in the Frogner district. Drive time from there is measured at Oslo 6 minutes, Sandvika 16, Bærum 22, Lillestrøm 43 and Asker 50 minutes. The outermost municipality sits well within an hour's drive, so the whole area is realistic to deliver in.

Sandvika is the administrative centre of Bærum municipality and not a municipality of its own. It therefore sits inside Bærum when the area is set up, but is written out as its own place name in the copy, because that is how people in the area talk about where they live. A homeowner in Sandvika who sees his own place name reads it as us driving out to him.

Oslo has the most small houses in absolute numbers, 81,646. Bærum has the oldest small house stock, where 62.6 percent is from 1980 or earlier, and the area's most expensive detached houses outside Oslo. Asker has the most detached houses and the highest share within the target age range. Lillestrøm has the lowest property prices and a homeowner who reacts more to saved maintenance than to property value.

### Scarcity

The slot count is the main engine, and the homeowner's own photo date is the personal deadline that makes the number concrete for him specifically.

This is how it is phrased in ad and funnel: «Vi tar inn 15 fasadevask-jobber i Oslo, Bærum, Sandvika, Asker og Lillestrøm denne måneden. Er de fylt, står neste ledige oppstart etter at fotografen har vært hos deg, og da er bildene tatt på et hus som ikke er vasket.» ("We are taking in 15 facade washing jobs in Oslo, Bærum, Sandvika, Asker and Lillestrøm this month. If they are filled, the next available start falls after the photographer has been to you, and then the pictures have been taken of a house that has not been washed.")

Secondary variant for when the slot count starts to tire: «Vi er inne i siste vaskevindu før høsten setter inn. Sikre plass før fotografen din er booket.» ("We are in the last washing window before autumn sets in. Secure a slot before your photographer is booked.")

The number 15 is tuned to the target group and must stand as it is. It is credible for a small local operator with its own lift in an area with over 170,000 small houses, and low enough that it feels urgent without becoming absurd. The seasonal argument is genuine on top of that: August is the wettest month of the year in Oslo, so the facades look their worst right now, just before autumn's last washing window.
